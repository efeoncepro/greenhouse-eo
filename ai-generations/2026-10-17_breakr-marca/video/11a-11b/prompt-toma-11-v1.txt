Single continuous take in real time. No slow motion, no cuts. Photorealistic live action, cinema camera, 24 fps, 180-degree shutter: fast water reads as motion-blurred streaks, sheets and spray, never as round frozen beads.

Image 1 is the first frame. Image 2 is the exact BREAKR can (orange body, teal band, white vertical BREAKR lettering).

Fixed geography: the camera is behind the left shoulder of a man in a navy jacket, out of focus in the lower right corner. The back of his open laptop is at the bottom centre of frame; its screen faces the woman across the table. The laptop screen is the only source of water. The water travels along ONE straight line, from the top edge of the laptop lid up the frame and to the left, to the woman's chest, straight away from the camera. Nothing enters from the left, right or top edges of the frame.

The whole event is over in under one second, faster than she can react:
0.0-0.25 s: the low, heavy jet of water that is already pouring out over the top of the laptop lid keeps shooting across the table into her, like a bucket of water thrown at her; its white foaming front reaches her chest. The can is carried inside the jet, tumbling toward her. The camera starts a fast dolly forward behind the water.
0.25 s: the water slams into her chest; the camera jolts once.
0.25-0.7 s: the water bursts up in a crown over her chest, face and hair; her head snaps back slightly, eyes squeezed shut. The can bounces off her chest and flips up beside her head, spinning, then falls.
0.7-4.0 s: aftermath with real gravity, the camera settling on a medium shot of her. The splash falls back; water pours off her hair, chin and sweater onto the table; the can lands on its side on the wet table and lies still with its BREAKR label visible; orange slices lie on the wet wood. She stays frozen in shock and SOAKED until the last frame: hair dark and plastered to her face and neck, cream sweater dark and dripping, table glossy with water. She still holds her paper coffee cup.

The man in the foreground never moves or turns and stays dry. Her face, cup, the room (glass partition and open office on the left, wall with a dark screen on the right, no windows) and the warm low morning sun stay as in Image 1. The thin top and bottom bands are mirrored padding: keep them still.

Avoid: water entering from the sides; water rising out of the table; a hovering or standing mound of water; water slowing, pausing or vanishing; slow motion; frozen droplets or glass beads; any glow, flame, light trail or thrust under or behind the can; the can flying by itself or hovering near her head; her drying off; her hair changing colour, length or style; any cut or jump to another angle or room; text, subtitles, extra people.

Sound, no music: hard hiss of water rushing over wood, a heavy wet slap on impact, her short choked gasp, the hollow clank of the aluminium can landing, then dripping and quiet room tone.
